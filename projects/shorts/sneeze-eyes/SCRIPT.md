## VO (130 words, ~41 s at 3.2 w/s)

If you sneeze with your eyes open, your eyeballs pop right out of your head. At least, that's what every kid on the playground told you. All that pressure, a hundred miles an hour, boom. But your eyes aren't corks. Six muscles hold each eyeball in its socket, like ropes on a tent. And that hundred miles an hour? Scientists filmed real sneezes with high-speed cameras. About ten. Your eyes shut anyway because of a reflex, and nobody's totally sure why. Probably to keep your own germs out. So yes, you can sneeze with your eyes open. It just looks cursed. The real danger is holding it in. A man in Scotland pinched his nose, shut his mouth, and tore a hole in his windpipe. His eyes were fine though.

## Fact check (verified 2026-11-08)
- Eyes don't pop out from sneezing with them open. Six extraocular muscles anchor each eye. Sources: Naked Scientists, ScienceAlert, Newswise.
- Nobody knows for sure why we close our eyes when we sneeze; the leading theory is protection from germs and irritants.
- "100 mph" is a myth. Tang et al. (University of Alberta) filmed sneezes with high-speed imaging and measured a peak of 4.5 m/s, about 10 mph. MythBusters measured 35-39 mph, so "about ten" refers to the study specifically.
- Windpipe tear: BMJ Case Reports, Dec 2023, Ninewells Hospital, Dundee. A man in his 30s pinched his nose and shut his mouth while driving, causing a ~2 mm tracheal tear. He was treated without surgery.
